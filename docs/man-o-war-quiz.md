# Portuguese man o' war — quiz

Three questions, four answers each, Kahoot format. Designed for a mixed audience of
roughly ages seven to ninety, to be played after the audio guide or after reading the
object story.

Correct answers are marked. Every correct answer comes from Canterbury Museum sources.
The incorrect answers are invented, and deliberately plausible rather than silly, so
that younger players are not patronised and older players are not bored.

Total playing time about ninety seconds.

---

## Question 1

**A Portuguese man o' war looks like a single animal. What is it really?**

| | Answer | |
|---|---|---|
| Red | One very large jellyfish | |
| Blue | A kind of floating seaweed | |
| Yellow | A colony of many small animals living as one | **correct** |
| Green | A young octopus | |

Time limit: 30 seconds.

Opens on the biggest surprise in the story. "One very large jellyfish" is a deliberate
trap for anyone who heard "related to true jellyfish" and stopped listening at that
clause.

---

## Question 2

**Why couldn't the Museum display a real man o' war?**

| | Answer | |
|---|---|---|
| Red | They are too rare to catch | |
| Blue | They are too big for a display case | |
| Yellow | Preserving one strips away almost all its colour | **correct** |
| Green | They were protected by law in the 1800s | |

Time limit: 30 seconds.

The central idea of the whole story: the glass model exists because the animal itself
could not be kept. The correct answer uses the guide's concrete detail, colour loss,
rather than an abstract statement about preservation.

All four options are the same grammatical shape and similar length, so no one can win
by spotting the odd one out.

---

## Question 3

**Every man o' war is either left-handed or right-handed. What decides which?**

| | Answer | |
|---|---|---|
| Red | The way its sail curves | **correct** |
| Blue | Which tentacle grew first | |
| Yellow | The ocean it was born in | |
| Green | Which way it spins in the water | |

Time limit: 30 seconds.

Ends on the fact players are most likely to repeat to someone else afterwards. All
three incorrect answers are plausible enough that guessing does not pay.

## Design notes

**The arc** is the story compressed: what is it, why is it glass, one last strange
thing. Same spine as the audio guide, in ninety seconds rather than four minutes.

**Character limits.** All question stems are under 55 characters and all answers under
50. Kahoot allows 120 and 75 respectively, leaving room if the quiz is ever translated,
since most European languages run longer than English.

**One deliberate omission.** "They are dangerous to touch" was rejected as a distractor
for Question 2. It is the most tempting wrong answer and would catch many players, but
it is also true, and stinging was cut from the audio guide because the Museum's man o'
war entry does not mention it. Anyone choosing it would be penalised for outside
knowledge rather than for not listening.

**Rejected question candidates**, and why:

- Dates and model counts, such as the 1882 order or the 630 models listed by 1878.
  Pure recall, and dull at both ends of the age range.
- "What is it made of?" Visible from the object itself, so it tests nothing.
- The European Decorative Arts classification. The sharpest idea in the project, but it
  requires understanding how museums file things, which is beyond a seven-year-old.
- The 27 October 1883 *Press* review. Too subtle to test in twenty seconds.
- "How many long tentacles does it have?" A good question, and the only one answerable
  by looking rather than remembering. Held in reserve as an alternative opener if a
  gentler warm-up is ever wanted.
